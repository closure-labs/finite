# Desktop risk policy

Finite uses Bluefin/bootc with Nix and Home Manager, not NixOS. This policy covers all four desktop profiles and their development/container tools: hostile websites, downloads, archives and images; local privilege escalation; VPN and remote-service exposure; and attacks on development dependencies. SELinux remains required, but it does not establish that a vulnerable program cannot expose user files, credentials, authentication decisions or an affected container boundary.

The `upstream-maintained-risk` mode assigns ordinary inherited scanner findings to authenticated upstream maintenance. It does not require every fixable High finding to disappear before a candidate can qualify. Instead it blocks known exploitation, reviewed serious impacts and Critical findings lacking sufficient review. The severity-only `blockedFixableSeverities` field is retained for legacy policy/history and is not the decision rule in this mode. Ordinary advisory findings remain visible; accepting their upstream maintenance is a risk decision, not a claim that their code has been fixed.

The baseline must be independently authenticated by immutable identity, approved publisher and signature evidence. A policy mode string, an image label or a successful build is insufficient. Finite owns its changes, including kernel replacements, separately sourced Cosign, package floors and build scripts. Trust in the Bluefin baseline does not automatically authenticate these overrides or clear their vulnerability findings. Fresh database/provider coverage, distribution binding, artifact identity, complete match locations and signed-source checks continue to apply. Probe errors remain errors, not policy blockers or clearance.

## Blocking rules

1. CISA KEV findings block without exception, regardless of severity, upstream ownership or fix availability.
2. A match to a reviewed serious alias group blocks regardless of severity or whether a fix is published. The registry distinguishes desktop takeover, privilege escalation, container escape, authentication or TLS identity bypass, major data loss, and a named unresolved serious impact. Conditional exposure stays explicit until shipped-artifact proof resolves it.
3. A Critical finding without a qualifying artifact-scoped review blocks pending review, even if its publisher description suggests a process-only denial of service or a different platform. A low-quality or incomplete platform assertion cannot clear it.
4. Ordinary findings remain advisory under the independently authenticated baseline. This covers routine process crashes/resource exhaustion without a demonstrated serious boundary violation; it is not a general exemption for all denial of service. Host/kernel outages, backup destruction, credential leakage and exploitable memory corruption require separate consideration.

The serious registry is maintained as advisories change. It is not limited to zero-days or CVSS Critical. The current edition includes High findings with concrete file-write escapes, build/toolchain execution, container violations and authorization bypass. It also records uncertain memory-safety impacts and a publisher-described serious HPLIP family rather than quietly treating missing detail as harmless.

## Positive evidence and dispositions

There are **no active reviewed dispositions** and no new suppressions in this change. A future `nonapplicable`, `backported` or `routine` disposition must bind the reviewed aliases, exact candidate digest, exact versioned component, `linux/amd64` platform and every observed canonical in-image `locations` path. All path/accessPath/realPath aliases must be covered after removal of the verified native source prefix. Missing, unknown or traversal paths cannot be qualified by proof for one convenient file. New observed advisory aliases invalidate a narrower proof; serious groups remain independent of package version.

The record also requires a concrete reason, official sources, positive artifact evidence, review identity/time and expiry within 30 days. It must not waive KEV; `routine` cannot waive a serious group. Nonapplicability needs proof of the affected OS/function/use boundary for every shipped match, including all supported profiles. Backports need exact source/patch and shipped-package binding; upstream release notes or a higher package version alone are insufficient. A negative reachability analyzer, an absent finding or an updated Go compiler does not establish that already compiled embedded Go modules were repaired.
